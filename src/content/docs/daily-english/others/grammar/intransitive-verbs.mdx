          <span class="text-body font-bold">What is an intransitive verb?</span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight">
          is a verb that <mark class="font-bold">doesn't take a direct object</mark>, 
          <mark class="bg-sky-200 font-bold">the action or state stays with the subject</mark> **instead of passing to another person or thing**.  
          - The package arrived.
          - She jumped.
          - He gave up.
          - A problem occurred.
          - She slept peacefully.
       </div>
      </li>

          <span class="text-body font-bold">How to identify an intransitive verb?</span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight">
          The quickest test is to ask 
          <mark class="bg-lime-300 font-bold">what?</mark> or 
          <mark class="bg-lime-300 font-bold">whom?</mark> after the verb.  
          **If no direct answer makes sense, the verb is intransitive**.  

          **Extra words after the verb do not automatically create an object**.   
          **The extra detail <mark class="bg-lime-200">adds meaning</mark>, but it does <mark>not</mark> turn the verb <mark>into a transitive verb</mark>**.

          In <mark class="bg-pink-200 font-bold">They waited outside</mark>, the word outside tells where they waited. It does not receive the action.  
        </div>

          <span class="text-body font-bold">Intransitive Verbs - <mark class="bg-sky-300">Group 1</mark> - <mark>Movement And Position Verbs</mark></span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight font-bold">
          Movement verbs often show where someone or something <span class="bg-green-300">goes, rises, falls, sits, or stands</span>.  
          The words after the verb usually tell <span class="bg-green-300">place or direction</span>.
          - She <span class="bg-lime-300">walked</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">across the bridge</span>.
          - They <span class="bg-lime-300">ran</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">toward the gate</span>.
          - The balloon <span class="bg-lime-300">floated</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">away</span>.
          - He <span class="bg-lime-300">sat</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">beside the window</span>.
       </div>
      </li>

        <div class="flex items-center">
          <svg class="w-8 h-8 shrink-0 text-fg-brand me-1.5" aria-hidden="true" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" fill="none" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path stroke="currentColor" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" stroke-width="2" d="M8.5 11.5 11 14l4-4m6 2a9 9 0 1 1-18 0 9 9 0 0 1 18 0Z"/></svg>
          <span class="text-body font-bold">Intransitive Verbs - <mark class="bg-sky-300">Group 2</mark> - <mark>Time, Change, And State Verbs</mark></span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight font-bold">
          Some intransitive verbs show what happens <mark>over time</mark>.   
          <mark class="bg-lime-300">A person grows, a color fades, a problem occurs, or a condition improves</mark>.   
          Something changes, <mark class="bg-red-600 text-white">but no direct object receives the action</mark>.
          - The child <span class="bg-lime-300">grew</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">quickly</span>.
          - The meeting <span class="bg-lime-300">began</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">late</span>.
          - The pain <span class="bg-lime-300">faded</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">after an hour</span>.
          - The plan <span class="bg-lime-300">improved</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">with time</span>.  

          Change verbs can feel tricky because something is happening, but that does not mean the verb has an object. 
       </div>
      </li>

        <div class="flex items-center">

          <span class="text-body font-bold">Intransitive Verbs - <mark class="bg-sky-300">Group 3</mark> - <mark>Weather And Nature Verbs</mark></span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight font-bold">
          Weather verbs are usually intransitive because the weather action does not pass to a direct object.   
          Many of them use <mark class="p-2">it</mark> as the subject, as in <mark class="bg-lime-300">It rained or It snowed</mark>.
          - It <span class="bg-lime-300">rained</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">all night</span>.
          - It <span class="bg-lime-300">snowed</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">for hours</span>.
          - Thunder <span class="bg-lime-300">roared</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">above the hills</span>.
          - The river <span class="bg-lime-300">flooded</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">after the storm</span>.  
          - The ground <span class="bg-lime-300">froze</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">overnight</span>.  

          In weather sentences, <mark class="p-2 ">it</mark> often fills the subject position.   
          The verb still does not need an object after it.
       </div>

          Many sound and body-action verbs describe what the subject does.   
          You can add <mark class="bg-sky-300">where, when, or how</mark> the action happened, but the verb still does not need something to receive the action.

          - The dog <span class="bg-lime-300">barked</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">at night</span>.  
          - She <span class="bg-lime-300">smiled</span> <span class="ml-2 bg-sky-300 ">at the news</span>.  

        </div>

          <span class="text-body font-bold">Quick Check For Intransitive Verbs</span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight font-bold">
          <mark>Use this short check when a sentence feels confusing:</mark>
            1. Find the verb.
            1. Ask <mark class="bg-lime-300">what? or whom?</mark> after the verb.
            1. Look for a direct answer.
            1. If there is <mark class="bg-violet-300">no direct object, the verb is intransitive</mark>.
            1. If the words after the verb show time, place, direction, or manner, they are extra details, not objects.
