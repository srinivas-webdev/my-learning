          <span class="text-body font-bold">Noun Clause</span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight">
          A <mark class="font-bold">noun clause</mark> can 
          <mark class="font-bold">begin with the word that or a wh-word</mark>.   
          The word <mark class="bg-red-300 font-bold">that</mark> 
          has no meaning in these clauses, and it is 
          <mark class="bg-red-300 font-bold">often omitted</mark>, especially in conversation.
          - I <mark class="bg-sky-300 font-bold">think (that)</mark> he is sick today. 
          - I <mark class="bg-sky-300 font-bold">wonder where</mark> you are.
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="flex flex-col">
        <div class="flex items-center">
          <svg class="w-8 h-8 shrink-0 text-fg-brand me-1.5" aria-hidden="true" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" fill="none" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path stroke="currentColor" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" stroke-width="2" d="M8.5 11.5 11 14l4-4m6 2a9 9 0 1 1-18 0 9 9 0 0 1 18 0Z"/></svg>
          <span class="text-body font-bold">What does the Research show?</span>
        </div> 
        <div class="ml-10 leading-tight">
          In **conversation**, <mark class="bg-lime-200 font-bold">verb + noun clause</mark>
          is commonly used to 
          <mark class="bg-lime-200 font-bold">express a lack of certainty about an idea</mark>.  
          The verb expresses the lack of certainty, and the noun clause expresses the idea.  
          When ideas are certain, speakers usually state them as a simple sentence (without a verb + noun clause structure).
        </div>
        <div class="ml-12 leading-tight flex flex-col font-bold">
          <span class="grid grid-cols-[1fr_2fr] gap-4 content-center">
            <span class="flex justify-end text-end">LACK OF CERTAINTY:</span>
            <span>I <mark class="bg-green-200 italic">think</mark> the stores close at four.</span>
          </span>
          <span class="grid grid-cols-[1fr_2fr] gap-4 content-center">
            <span class="flex justify-end text-end">CERTAIN:</span>
            <span>The stores close at four.</span>
          </span>
        </div>
      </li>

            used with that-clauses have  
            <mark class="bg-green-200">literal meanings that refer to mental processes</mark> 
            (such as thinking),  
            but they are used to
            <mark class="bg-green-200">express different levels of doubt/certainty:</mark>.  
          </span>
        </div>

            <ul class="w-full pl-6 mt-4 list-disc border-r-1 border-r-gray-300 flex flex-col gap-4">
              <li class="leading-tight">expresses <mark class="bg-green-300 italic">more certainty than doubt</mark>.</li>
              <li class="leading-tight">also used to express an <mark class="bg-green-300 italic">opinion</mark>.  
              <mark>_I think_</mark> or <mark>_I thought_</mark> can mean "in my opinion." </li>
              <li class="leading-tight"><mark class="bg-pink-300 italic">I think you should</mark> is often used to give <mark class="bg-green-300 italic">advice</mark>.</li>
            </ul>
            <ul class="w-full pl-6 list-disc flex flex-col gap-4">
              <li class="leading-tight">I <mark class="bg-green-300 italic">think</mark> the bowling fee includes the shoe rental too, but I'm not sure.</li>
              <li class="leading-tight">I <mark class=" italic">thought[= In my opinion]</mark> it looked pretty cool.</li>
              <li class="leading-tight">I <mark class="bg-pink-300 italic">think you should</mark> give this to Bob. [advice]</li>

              <mark class="italic">I think</mark> X is <mark class="italic">not</mark> true.</li>
              <li class="leading-tight">uses are similar to think (including <mark class="bg-green-300 italic">opinions</mark> and 
              <mark class="bg-green-300 italic">advice)</mark>.</li>
            </ul>
            <ul class="w-full pl-6 list-disc flex flex-col gap-4">

              <li class="leading-tight">very <mark>common in American English</mark> but rare in British English.</li>
